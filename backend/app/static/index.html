What is AQI and Why Do NO₂ & O₃ Numbers Matter?

Air Quality Index (AQI) is a standard government score from 0 to 500 translating complex photochemical concentrations into an actionable health risk score:

NO₂ (Nitrogen Dioxide): Emitted by diesel exhaust & vehicular traffic. Causes airway inflammation and asthma. Safe CPCB 24h standard: below 80 µg/m³.
O₃ (Ground-Level Ozone): Formed via daytime photolysis of vehicular precursors under solar UV. Safe CPCB 8h standard: below 100 µg/m³.
0 - 50: Good
51 - 100: Satisfactory
101 - 200: Moderate
201 - 300: Poor
301 - 400+: Severe

Atmospheric Chemistry & Photochemical Mechanics (Domain Rationale)

Photochemical Titration & Non-Linear Chemical Coupling: When commercial traffic decreases, primary vehicular NO emissions drop immediately. In urban VOC-limited regimes like Central Delhi, lower NO emissions reduce nocturnal Ozone titration (NO + O₃ -> NO₂ + O₂), leading to a significant reduction in toxic NO₂ while stabilizing afternoon Ozone formation.

CAQM Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) Enforcement Framework

Statutory regulatory directives triggered automatically when multi-horizon concentrations breach CPCB thresholds.

STAGE-I: POOR (AQI 201-300) TRIGGER: 80 µg/m³ NO₂

Strict enforcement of dust mitigation at construction sites; intensified water sprinkling; unannounced industrial stack inspections.

STAGE-II: VERY POOR (AQI 301-400) TRIGGER: 180 µg/m³ NO₂

Mechanized sweeping on designated arterial roads; deployment of mobile anti-smog guns; synchronized traffic lights to reduce idling.

STAGE-III: SEVERE (AQI 401-450) TRIGGER: 280 µg/m³ NO₂

Strict prohibition on plying of BS-III petrol and BS-IV diesel light motor vehicles; closure of brick kilns and stone crushers.

STAGE-IV: SEVERE+ (AQI > 450) TRIGGER: 400 µg/m³ NO₂

Complete ban on entry of non-essential commercial diesel trucks into Delhi NCR; emergency shift to remote work and school closures.
